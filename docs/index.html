    Heraldic blazon for Tailwind CSS

    Grailwind is a plugin for Tailwind CSS that lets you render coats of arms on your webpages using the ancient language of the heralds.

    Note: Grailwind is a light-hearted project at version 0.x. It's far from perfect, but I hope you at least find it fun!

    Install

    Grailwind is a plugin for Tailwind CSS v4.

    npm install -D grailwind

    Import it after Tailwind in your stylesheet:

    @import "tailwindcss";
@import "grailwind";

    This adds the heraldic tinctures alongside Tailwind's own colours, so bg-azure and bg-blue-500 both work.

    Strict mode

    To commit fully to the bit, import grailwind/strict instead. The tinctures then replace Tailwind's colours entirely, and bg-blue-500 no longer exists. This site uses strict mode. 

    @import "tailwindcss";
@import "grailwind/strict";

    Tinctures

    Heraldry has two metals and five colours. A tincture on its own sets the background, as it describes the field in blazon: <div class="azure">. Everywhere else, use Tailwind's usual prefixes: text-or, border-sable, and so on.

    The rule of tincture

    Even medieval heralds had visual contrast requirements. Never put a metal on a metal or a colour on a colour, so the design can be read from a distance. The ESLint plugin can flag this for you.

    Furs

    Furs are patterns: spots of one tincture on another. Use them like a tincture, on the field, an ordinary or a charge.

    In a divided field, a fur goes in either part, as in per-pale-ermine-gules (but presently only one of the two can be a fur). Generally, ermine and erminois count as metals, ermines and pean as colours.

    The shield

    escutcheon gives an element a shield shape. Size it however you like, e.g. escutcheon w-40.

    The top is the chief and the bottom is the base. Left and right are from the point of view of the person holding the shield. Dexter is their right, which is the left as you look at it, and sinister is their left, which to you is the right. It couldn't be simpler.

    You'll meet these words in the order of tinctures, in classes like bend-sinister, and in the named points for placing charges.

    Dividing the field

    One class paints a divided field. Any two different tinctures work. The first tincture named goes on the dexter side or in chief.

    Either tincture can be a fur, as in per-pale-ermine-gules.

    barry-* and paly-* are of six pieces. For more, use barry-of-eight-*, barry-of-ten-* or paly-of-eight-*. The same goes for bendy-*, bendy-sinister-* and chevronny-*, with -of-eight-*. chequy-* is five squares across and six down.

    Dividing for layout

    per-pale, per-fess and quarterly divide an element into equal parts, one child element per part, in blazon order. On a shield, per-fess and quarterly split at the fess point instead, a little above the middle. They're grids, so they also work for laying out a page. The top of this page uses md:per-pale.

    Bordure

    bordure-or, or any other tincture, adds a border. A canton or a chief goes over it.

    Inescutcheon

    inescutcheon is a small shield placed at the centre of the arms. It paints over everything else on the shield, and takes its own field, ordinaries and charges.

    Canton

    canton is a smaller field in dexter chief, a ninth of the shield. It counts as a later addition to the arms, so it paints over every ordinary and charge, a chief included. Like the inescutcheon, it goes beside the charges container and takes its own field, divisions, ordinaries and charges.

    Ordinaries

    The ordinaries are bold geometric shapes drawn over the field. Add a tincture to name one: fess-or, cross-gules. Bars, narrow fesses, are named by count: bar-or, two-bars-or and three-bars-or. Some have reversed variants. Any ordinary can also take a line: engrailed, invected, wavy, indented or embattled, as in fess-wavy-or, and most can be voided to leave just the outline.

    Embattled puts battlements on the upper edge only of a fess, bar, bend or chevron; every other ordinary has them on all its edges.

    The chief and the two and three bars can't be voided.

    Combining

    An element can have one ordinary plus a chief. With a chief, the other ordinary and any charges are drawn in the field below it. Ordinaries can go over any field, including a divided one.

    ☞ Fun fact: when a field has plain colours you can refer to its first tincture using -of-the-first, as in the pale above.

    Fimbriation

    Fimbriation adds a thin stripe around an ordinary. At present, only plain ordinaries can be fimbriated. Fimbriation for engrailed and fur ordinaries may be supported in a future release.

    Counterchanged

    On a divided field, a counterchanged ordinary has its tinctures mutually reversed against the surrounding field. It doesn't work with a field divided for layout, and it can't be fimbriated.

    Charges

    Charges are child elements inside a charges container, coloured with a tincture class.

    Charges can be counterchanged too: add counterchanged to a charge, or to the charges container for all of them. Don't rotate or scale a counterchanged charge, or it loses its place on the field. On a counterchanged ordinary, a charge is counterchanged against the ordinary.

    Arrangement

    Grailwind offers a few different options for placing charges: they can be automatically arranged on the field or between/on ordinaries, or placed explicitly at named points.

    In a line

    in-pale, in-fess, in-bend and in-bend-sinister put two or three charges in a vertical, horizontal or diagonal line.

    Between

    between places charges around the ordinary on the shield, whichever it is.

    On

    on places charges along the ordinary on the shield, whichever it is.

    At a named point

    Heraldry names nine points on the shield. Put one on a charge to place it there, as in in dexter chief a mullet.

    1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9
    1. in-dexter-chief dexter chief
    2. in-chief-point chief point
    3. in-sinister-chief sinister chief
    4. in-honour-point honour point
    5. in-fess-point fess point
    6. in-nombril-point nombril point
    7. in-dexter-base dexter base
    8. in-base-point base point
    9. in-sinister-base sinister base

    Points work alongside ordinaries and other charges. With a chief on the shield, the three chief points sit on the chief. in-chief and in-base arrange a container's charges along the chief or base.

    Sizing

    Each arrangement picks a sensible size. To change it, use charge-size-* with a percentage of the shield's width: charge-size-15, or any length, like charge-size-[2rem]. On the charges container it sizes every charge. On a single charge, just that one.

    Your own charges

    Any element inside charges gets placed, so you can bring your own. charge-[url(…)] uses your SVG as a mask, so a tincture class colours it. The URL is resolved relative to your built stylesheet, and the image must come from the same site or be served with CORS headers.

    An <img> keeps the image's own colours, like a charge proper.

    All together

    There is still plenty that Grailwind can't do, and it'll almost certainly never do everything! All the same, I hope you have fun playing around with it. Below are some more complex examples to stoke the imagination.

    Accessibility

    A shield is a picture made of empty elements. Give it a text alternative, ideally its blazon: 

    <div class="escutcheon azure fess-or" role="img"
     aria-label="Azure, a fess Or"></div>

    Grailwind uses :has(), CSS masks and CSS nesting, so it needs a current browser. Engrailed, invected and wavy edges use clip-path: shape(). Where that isn't supported, the ordinary is drawn with straight edges instead.

    Linting (beta)

    Some mistakes render quietly wrong. @grailwind/eslint-plugin catches them in JSX and HTML:

    • Metal on metal or colour on colour, including charges on an ordinary, and furs.
    • A charge outside a charges container.
    • between or on with no ordinary to place the charges.
    • Two ordinaries on one shield, or two arrangements on one container.
    • counterchanged or fimbriated-* with nothing to apply to. 
    npm install -D @grailwind/eslint-plugin@beta

    Then turn on its rules, here for JSX:

    // eslint.config.js
import grailwind from "@grailwind/eslint-plugin";

export default [
  {
    files: ["**/*.{jsx,tsx}"],
    languageOptions: { parserOptions: { ecmaFeatures: { jsx: true } } },
  },
  grailwind.configs.recommended,
];

    HTML setup, each rule and its options are in the plugin's README. It's a beta, so rules and options may change between releases.
